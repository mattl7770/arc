ARC · Eat tab · Layout proposal · Round 3

The Eat tab, redrawn as a tab root.

Eat became a tab on 09 Aug by a one-line re-export of the pushed nutrition hub — the route changed and the screen did not. This is the screen redrawn for the job, and widened to hold what nutrition now contains: the recipe book, the grocery list, link import, and the Coach’s eight new food tools.

Where it stands: the type scale is lifted a step and the metadata layer darkened for legibility; all logging collapses into one button that says Log; the macro cells are now boxed; recipe import moves into the recipe book; and the estimate screen gains a photo-library input. The sheets are drawn at half the shipped paper-grid weight, on the assumption that the background retune lands separately — everything else is the live Conformed Set: px-5 gutter, plates at px-3.5 py-3 on a 1px rule, sections on whitespace alone, one accent per screen. Every phone is a true 375 × 812, so the cut at the tab bar is the real fold.

What you asked for, round by round

  • “Put boxes for the nutrition info at the top.”The four macro cells are boxed — 1px rule on plate stock, a 9pt gutter between them. The hero stays unboxed: at 38px it holds its own ground, and five boxes would read as a slab. Sheet 00 keeps the unboxed grid for comparison.
  • “Upload a photo from my photo library for nutrition tracking.”A third input on the estimate screen — type it, take a photo, or choose one — feeding the pipeline that already exists: one model turn, grounded against the catalog, editable review, nothing written until you save. Sheet 06.
  • “Make it all a bit more readable.”Every type role goes up a step and the metadata layer darkens — section labels 10 → 11px, row titles 15 → 16px, detail lines 11 → 12–13px and off the muted ink onto ink-secondary, rows 44 → 46pt with more air.
  • “Condense all the logging into just one Log button.”One full-width accent button reading Log. No stamp, no fold, no sub-heading, no explanatory sentence. Every path lives inside it — sheet 05.
  • “Move the import recipe button into the recipe book screen.”Done — Kitchen is two rows now. Import is already the book’s own primary action, so the tab root stops carrying a third door to the same place.

Your original four notes, still answered

Leads with what’s left, not what’s eaten: 780 kcal left, with 1,620 of 2,400 kcal as the corner note, so the three figures reconcile on one line.

One obvious action instead of a five-item menu — now literally one word.

“Set daily targets” is a full-width control while targets are null, and retires the moment they exist.

“Over time” carries two real readings — 14-day energy and protein — so the tab can finally answer how a month has gone.

The honesty rule the hero has to pass

A day’s totals skip NULL by design — a meal logged without a kcal figure contributes nothing to the sum (sumRounded, nutrition.tsx:183). Eaten-so-far survives that; “what’s left” does not. Subtract an incomplete sum from a target and the tab prints a confident number that is simply too large, on the exact days its own ledger shows an em-dash.

So the remainder is computed per metric, and shown only when a target exists and every meal logged today carries a value for it. Otherwise that metric falls back to the reading that ships now — eaten, with its denominator — and one line says why. Sheet 06 is that state.

Sharing from Instagram — where it actually stands

Not yet, and it cannot be until you run an EAS build. A share extension is a new native target; it does not exist in the binary on your phone, so ARC will not appear in Instagram’s share sheet today.

Written and verified as far as is possible without a device: the extension is configured in app.json for text, links and images; the plugin chain evaluates cleanly and emits the target EAS provisions; the deep link routes to the import screen; the payload router (link beats link-in-text beats text beats screenshot) is pinned by tests. On the current binary it all no-ops rather than breaking.

Working right now: copy the link, open the recipe book, Import, paste. That path needs only the network and stays the permanent fallback.

Two caveats worth holding: Expo’s first-party receive API is experimental and cold/warm delivery is unproven on hardware (expo-share-intent is the recorded fallback), and the build needs regenerated provisioning — a new target plus an App Group entitlement, and the last attempt already failed on signing.

Calls that are yours to overrule

1 · “Kitchen” as the section name. A real-world noun for where food lives before it is a meal, and it leaves room for a pantry row later. If it reads as conceit vocabulary, the plain alternative is “Recipes & grocery”.

2 · The Log button is solid petrol, not a bordered stamp. One word wants weight; a 1.5px outline around the word “Log” would read as a spacer. It stays the tab’s only accent in every state — including first run, where “Set daily targets” is an outlined secondary above it.

3 · Log opens a full-screen sheet, not a menu that pushes a screen. Same shape as the mode picker and the exercise picker, so it dismisses with a close control and never adds a back-stack.

4 · Over time shows energy and protein, with no chooser. No chips, no window switcher — no tab root in this app has in-page sub-navigation, and the 7/30/90 chooser already lives one tap down in History.

5 · Boxing the cells departs from the shipped surface system, on your instruction. The grid device draws nothing today — the between-cell hairlines were cut in August because they read as a half-drawn box, and four bordered cells abreast is close to the shape you rejected on Home. Worth a look on hardware before it sticks; if it reads as noise, the fallback is boxing nothing and letting the lifted type scale carry the legibility on its own.

Out of scope, still true: the Eat tab stays mode-blind — a Sick day does not move these targets — which belongs to the Modes item. And the five recipe/grocery screens on the feature branch are still drawn in Porcelain Ledger; they need a Conformed Set restyle in the same pass that builds this, since that branch was cut before the restyle landed.

07 · When the data hasn’t earned a remainder

A meal was logged by name with no numbers — so “what’s left” cannot be computed, because the day’s sums skip what was never recorded. Rather than print a confident 1,040 that is wrong by a whole dinner, the tab falls back to exactly the reading that ships today — eaten, its denominator, its progress rules — and one line says why. The em-dash in the ledger and the missing remainder above are the same fact, stated twice. Fill the meal in and the sheet becomes 01.
