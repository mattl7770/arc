# Critical claim ledger

Date checked: 2026-07-28

This is the claim-level appendix for the priority UI/UX brief. It records what
the source supports, what it does not, and where CareerPigeon-specific judgment
begins.

| Claim | Tier | Source | Direct support | Limitation | CareerPigeon synthesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A dashboard needs a deliberate hierarchy and should limit nonessential metrics | B | [Carbon dashboards](https://carbondesignsystem.com/data-visualization/dashboards/) | Dashboard guidance says context determines design, important information should dominate, and nonessential metrics should be limited | Carbon is enterprise guidance, not CareerPigeon user research | Make the next-action queue dominant and progress secondary |
| A personal home can curate work by urgency and relevance rather than show everything equally | C | [Linear My Issues](https://linear.app/docs/my-issues), [Asana My Tasks](https://asana.com/features/project-management/my-tasks) | Both document curated personal work views and prioritization | Product examples do not prove fit for job seekers | Use a short cross-module action queue, then test its grouping with users |
| Incoming items can remain outside active work until reviewed | C | [Linear Triage](https://linear.app/docs/triage) | Linear separates intake and supports accept, decline, duplicate, and snooze | Issue intake differs from job opportunities | Apply the model to recruiter recommendations and imported applications |
| One data model can support multiple task views | C | [Notion views](https://www.notion.com/help/views-filters-and-sorts), [Asana project views](https://asana.com/features/project-management/project-views) | Both document list, board, calendar, timeline, filtering, and sorting over shared work | Flexibility can add complexity and does not justify every view | Applications may use list and board; each other module needs only task-justified views |
| A focused interview-practice room can keep the current question and recording controls dominant | C | [Prepra](https://www.prepraai.com/) | The product precedent visibly centers one question, a recording state, and a small control set | A marketing demonstration does not establish usability, feedback validity, or trust | Use it only as a visual hierarchy precedent; validate recording comprehension and ground feedback in CareerPigeon transcript evidence |
| A networking workspace can lead with relationship history rather than pipeline state | C | [Dex](https://getdex.com/) | The product precedent visibly prioritizes a person record, relationship note, recency, and reminders while rejecting sales-pipeline framing | It does not prove the right data model or reminder cadence for job seekers | Put relationship context before any generated draft, keep reminders user-controlled, and prohibit auto-send behavior |
| Onboarding should ask only necessary questions and begin with one decision per page | A/B | [GOV.UK form structure](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure), [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/) | Guidance explicitly supports necessary questions and starting with one thing per page | “One per page” is a starting point, not an immutable rule | Use one meaningful decision at a time and group only when comprehension remains clear |
| Generated artifacts need human review and efficient correction | B | [Microsoft HAX correction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/guideline/support-efficient-correction/), [Google PAIR feedback](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/feedback-controls/) | Guidance supports granular correction, controls, and clear feedback effects | It does not prescribe a resume-editor layout | Use claim-level diff, provenance, accept/edit/reject/restore, and explicit export approval |
| Local explanations are more useful than a generic AI rationale | B | [Google PAIR explainability](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/explainability-trust/), [Microsoft HAX explanations](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/guideline/make-clear-why-the-system-did-what-it-did/) | Guidance supports explanations tied to inputs, outputs, and user context | Explanation quality still requires domain testing | Anchor explanation to the exact resume claim, recommendation, or interview excerpt |
| A SaaS landing page should show concrete product UI | C | [Baymard on showing SaaS UI](https://baymard.com/blog/highlight-saas-ui) | Commercial UX research argues that product imagery helps prospects understand software | It does not determine CareerPigeon’s art direction | Put a truthful connected-workflow artifact near the first fold |
| Public pages should be structured around a user need and next action | A/B | [GOV.UK user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) | Guidance requires services and content to begin from evidenced user needs | Government-service framing differs from marketing | Lead with the candidate’s job-search outcome, not a module list |
| Article authorship and publication dates should be visible and consistent | A/C | [Google Article data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article), [GOV.UK content maintenance](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/manage-existing-govuk-content/) | Sources support author/date metadata and maintained content | Metadata does not create editorial trust by itself | Make authorship, freshness, sources, and corrections part of the visible article system |
| Accessibility conformance requires complete pages and processes, not selected checks | A | [WCAG 2.2 conformance](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#conformance-reqs) | WCAG defines full-page and complete-process conformance | Conformance does not guarantee usability or visual quality | Maintain a complete applicable A/AA matrix and test representative assistive-technology workflows |
| AI-generated interface work can be usable yet visually conventional | D | [Usable but Conventional](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15124) | Preprint reports usable prototypes with weak originality/innovation ratings | Preprint, limited scope, not a universal taxonomy | Treat functional polish as insufficient; require product-specific hierarchy and visual judgment |
| Common “AI slop” patterns are heuristics, not proven universal failures | D | [SmoothUI](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop), [Impeccable](https://impeccable.style/slop/) | Practitioner catalogs identify recurring visual conventions | Opinionated catalogs; no causal proof | Use patterns as reviewer prompts and test purpose, not as automatic bans |
| Anti-slop review should combine design-system drift checks, repeatable CLI/browser detectors, and contextual critique | D | [Impeccable slop catalog](https://impeccable.style/slop/), dated entry in `04-evidence-ledger-and-precedent-map.md` | The page catalogs 64 patterns, says its detector covers 59 enabled rules across source and rendered pages, and says five broader judgments remain in critique; it separates personalized CLI, deterministic CLI/browser, and LLM-only review modes | Detection catches signatures and defects, not whether a choice earns its place in a specific product; the practitioner catalog is not settled design science | Run design-system and deterministic checks for repeatable defects, then require fresh human review of product purpose, task fit, accessibility, and intentional exceptions |

## Corrections to avoid evidence overreach

- The job-search progress study supports a relationship between perceived
  progress and later search intensity; it does not prove a specific
  CareerPigeon metric set. The proposed quality signals are synthesis.
- Duolingo’s streak experiment is evidence about retention mechanics, not proof
  that every streak or leaderboard is harmful. CareerPigeon’s anti-pressure
  position is a product-value decision to validate with users.
- Chrome’s documentation supports narrow purpose, permissions, and disclosure.
  The preview-correct-save extension sequence is CareerPigeon synthesis.
- OpenAI’s Model Spec is a living document. Interview-design requirements
  should rest primarily on observable-evidence and contestability principles,
  not a dated Model Spec snapshot.
