> ✅ **THE MUSCLE FIGURE, SIXTH ATTEMPT — REAL SVG, 2026-08-25 (branch `claude/figure-svg`).** *"Current one still looks meh, fix it again."* Five rejections, all of them drawn out of filled `View`s. **The constraint that forced that is gone** — the owner has rebuilt and called the build cost low — so `react-native-svg` **15.15.4** is in, the one new dependency, and every mark in the figure is now a `<Path>`. No migration. Gate: `npm run db:test` **3,277 assertions / 53 suites, 0 failed** (`screens-render` 400 → **416**), `db:validate` 20/20, `tsc` 0, `eslint` 0 errors, `npx expo export --platform ios` clean **with the new native dependency in the graph** (6.3 MB iOS bundle). ⚠️ **Headless only — not seen on a device, and it needs the next EAS build to appear at all.**
>
> **1. What SVG had to buy, and did: internal shading.** The ceiling the `View` version hit was not smoothness, it was that a `View` is ONE FLAT COLOUR. Every muscle was a single tint inside a single ink line, so the drawing read as a coloured **map** — regions labelled by shade — rather than as a rendering of a body. Anatomy plates get their depth from the gradient *within* each belly. Every muscle now carries one, built from three primitives chosen by what the muscle is: **`belly`** (a cylinder — dark border, lit crest, dark border, across the fibres) for the pec, the deltoid straps, the obliques, the two quad masses, the two hamstring masses, the lats and the erector columns; **`dome`** (a lit core falling to a dark rim, in object-bounding-box units so the highlight stretches with the shape) for the glutes, the deltoid caps, each of the eight rectus segments, the bicep/tricep spindles, the forearms and the calf bellies; and **`furrow`** (two crests either side of a dark valley) for the trapezius yoke, which is what draws the spine down the middle of a back. The erectors get the same furrow from a mirrored pair whose dark medial ends meet at the midline. `db/exercise-ai.test.mjs` asserts every muscle carries a modelled gradient and that mirrored pairs are mirror-symmetric in their SHADING as well as their outline — lighting the left quad from the groove and the right one from the flank makes one leg look twisted.
>
> **2. The geometry was PORTED, not restarted.** The 34-region anatomical layout is four rounds of iteration and was kept: a shape is now a **closed centripetal Catmull–Rom spline** through the same anchors (`Contour`), emitted as cubic beziers by `pathD`. Two tensions, and the split is anatomical rather than aesthetic — at tension 0 every shape balloons into an oval (the pec loses its sternal border, the trap loses its four points, the abs become circles), so **muscles run at 0.34** to keep their corners and **the body at 0.12** because a silhouette genuinely has no corners in it. Real curves also retired the whole scanline apparatus: no `polyBars`, no adaptive bar counts, no inflated ink copies. **The figure pair costs 168 SVG nodes at every size, against 1,168 native views at 128pt** — and the old count *moved with the render size*, because a rasterised polygon's bar count did.
>
> **3. Shading is BUDGETED, and that is the one place the round nearly broke something.** A highlight needs headroom above the fill, and the spent end of the ramp has none: `#8F8978` is exactly 3.14:1 on the plate. The first fix darkened `MUSCLE_SPENT` to make room — and the suite showed the cost: the ramp's ends had been **2.36:1 apart in luminance**, which is what carries the reading for a hue-blind reader, and darkening collapsed that to **1.69:1**. A brighter drawing a colourblind reader can no longer read is not an improvement. So the ramp is untouched and the **highlight yields instead** (`liftBudget`): each reading is lightened only as far as `CONTRAST_FLOOR` allows, so a dark reading takes the full lift and the spent end takes none and is modelled by its shadow alone — which is what a light mass wants anyway. **Every published number survives: 0 → 3.14:1 · 50 → 3.37:1 · 100 → 4.71:1**, and those are now measured at each muscle's **brightest pixel** rather than at a nominal fill, which is strictly stronger than the flat version could claim. Head, hands and feet stay the PLATE colour at 3.14:1 from a spent muscle, and are additionally the only surfaces in the drawing with no gradient — "not data" said twice.
>
> **4. The tiling invariant survived the port, and the floors went UP.** `coveredByBody` (containment) and the per-band coverage floors added last round — because containment is satisfied by pills floating in a body and passed all four earlier rejections — both still run, over `flatten()`, which turns a bezier back into a dense polygon. Two new bands joined them and the floors rose: **trunk 62 → 74, thigh 65 → 74, plus shoulder 78 and arm 70**, measured at **front 81/84/82/78, back 83/76/73/76**. The lever that bought it is `MUSCLE_GROW`, one central offset along each contour's own vertex normals, **clamped per-anchor by bisection against the silhouette** — growing uniformly pushed eight shapes off the body at once, because a bicep and a calf belly are already flush with the limb and have nowhere to go while a pec has a unit of slack.
>
> **5. `db/figure-preview.mjs` was rewritten for paths and earned its keep again.** It flattens through the same `flatten()` the assertions use, fills by even-odd scanline with a **per-pixel gradient** (SVG's `objectBoundingBox` semantics), and strokes by distance-to-edge at the real rendered point weight. Nine iterations; the ones that mattered: an **inverted Catmull–Rom control-point sign** that shredded every contour into spikes; **tension 0 turning the whole figure into ovals**; the shading being **invisible at the fresh end** (which is what forced the highlight, since `signalInk.optimal` is a colour picked to clear 4.5:1 as *text* and is already nearly black); a **light-bulb head with a pointed chin cutting a V across an over-wide neck**; a hard-edged **plate-coloured rectangle between the thighs**, where the torso's flat lower border exposed the background; and, at the hub's real 118pt, an **ink DOT in the crotch** where the two thighs sat 2.2 units apart and the silhouette's double-weight contour stroke met itself with no fill to cover it. None of those six is visible in any assertion.
>
> **6. Stroke weight is specified in POINTS, not grid units** (`strokeUnits`), because the same geometry serves 72 / 118 / 128pt and a width fixed in grid units renders 1.8× heavier at the top of that range: the hub would be a woodcut and exercise-detail would have no contour. The silhouette holds 1.15pt, a muscle 0.62pt, the wrist/ankle/jaw lines 0.55pt. The freshness scale bar is a **true gradient** now rather than fourteen stacked bands — a stepped key to a continuous ramp is a key to something else.
>
> **7. The render suite is stubbed, and the package's own web build was tried first.** `react-native-svg` ships one, and reaching it needs Metro's platform-extension resolution (`./elements` → `elements.web.js`); that was implemented in `db/render-hook.mjs` and worked twice before the import graph walked into `@react-native/assets-registry`, which has no web entry at all. Teaching a test resolver enough of Metro to load a native SVG library is larger and more fragile than the suite it serves, so `db/render-stubs/react-native-svg.mjs` maps every element to its **real DOM SVG tag** instead — `renderToString` emits genuine `<path d="M…C…Z" fill="url(#…)">`, so a malformed path or a mismatched gradient id shows up in the HTML rather than being swallowed. All three figure screens are on the render walk — and **`app/exercise-detail.tsx` joined it for the first time**, since the figure’s `mode: 'muscles'` firewall (a fact about an exercise must never wear the signal green) was the one rule in this area with no test on it; the blocker had been `images.generated.ts`’s 69 static `require('…jpg')` calls, which are a resolve-time ReferenceError under ESM, so that got a stub too. A new `figureDrew` assertion catches the three failures a text check walks past: a figure that mounted **no paths**, a `url(#…)` fill pointing at an id that is not in the document (black on iOS, nothing on web), and a **NaN** in any coordinate. **416 render assertions pass, up from 400, none deleted.**
>
> ⚠️ **No guarded `require` seam, and that is a traced decision rather than an oversight.** Expo Router eagerly requires every route file, so an unguarded native import under `app/` is an app-STARTUP crash — it has shipped twice. `react-native-svg`'s two `TurboModuleRegistry.getEnforcing` calls are both **lazy, inside methods** (`elements/Shape.js`, `elements/Svg.js`), and `codegenNativeComponent` is lazy under Fabric, so importing the package touches no native module and startup is safe. What is NOT safe is rendering `<Svg>` on a binary without it: `runtimeVersion` is `appVersion` and the app version is unchanged, so **this must not be pushed as an OTA update to the current 0.2.0 binary** — it ships with the build or not at all.
>
> ⚠️ **Weaknesses to judge on hardware:** the head, hands and feet are the plate colour and read as *empty* rather than as neutral parts — that is forced, since 3.14:1 against a spent muscle is achieved exactly at the plate and there is no room to darken them; the lower legs still show bare ground beside the calf bellies (the shin and the achilles genuinely carry nothing in this 16-muscle taxonomy, but that band still scores lowest); the clavicle strip above the pecs is bare because the upper trapezius is a back-view muscle in this taxonomy; and the whole drawing is a dark, saturated green at the fresh end because `signalInk.optimal` was cut for text contrast, not for filling area — the highlight is what makes it read at all.

> ✅ **THE STRAIN ROUND, 2026-08-25 (branch `claude/strain-arc`). NO MIGRATION** — head stays `0042`; strain is a derivation, not storage. > **1. Strain is computed from ARC's own data now** (owner: *"switch to ARC computing"*). It was `yesterday's active_energy_kcal ÷ the mean of the prior 29 days`, and **active energy is a calorie-burn proxy while a hard resistance session burns few calories** — so on the morning after a back day, Home's Strain pillar read `optimal / fresh` three inches above a muscle figure reporting lats at **27%**. Two readings of one body, on one screen, contradicting each other. Strain now reads `dailyMuscleSetLoad` over `workout_sets` — **the same substrate the freshness engine reads**, which is exactly why the two can no longer disagree — and it needs no HealthKit and no network.
>
> The ratio is **yesterday's role-weighted sets ÷ the mean over prior TRAINING days** in the 30 days ending yesterday. Training days, not calendar days, and that is the load-bearing choice: averaging rest days into the denominator would put a typical session on a four-day split at ~1.75× and read `poor` every time it happened. Over training days, **1.0 is your average session** — which is what the retired energy ratio also meant, so **the thresholds did not move** (≤0.75 optimal · ≤1.30 good · ≤1.70 caution · else poor). The calibration is now a specification rather than a guess: rest day 0.00 `optimal` · 6-set accessory day 0.38 `optimal` · 12-set usual session 1.00 `good` · 17-set back day 1.42 `caution` · 25-set marathon 2.08 `poor`, each pinned in `db/readiness.test.mjs` §6 alongside the defect itself (a 17-set back day with active energy at 0.6× must read `caution`).
>
> **Active energy survives as a second opinion, under `max()`, and that is one-directional by construction:** it can add strain the sets could not see (a two-hour hike) and can never subtract strain the sets did see. A blend would have averaged a hard lifting day's 1.0 against its unremarkable 0.85 of calories back down to 0.93 — the retired defect in a new coat. **ARC's volume is primary in the strict sense:** without a sets baseline there is no verdict even with a year of energy sitting there, because energy alone is the reading the owner rejected.
>
> **The evidence gate counts SESSIONS, not days** — `STRAIN_MIN_SESSIONS` = 5 prior logged workouts, because a rest day teaches a session baseline nothing. On a four-day split that is a little over a fortnight, and the pillar says how many sessions are left while it fills. ⚠️ **Strain's note no longer mentions Apple Health at all**, and a test asserts it cannot: an absent HealthKit module is not why strain is blank, and offering the Settings switch as the fix would send the reader somewhere that changes nothing.

Home's mode picker had it in the trailing corner; the exercise picker led but at −8 rather than −12, so its glyph sat two points inside every other leading control in the app. All three now carry `StackHeader`'s control verbatim — `-ml-3`, a 44×44 target, a 22pt glyph in `palette.ink` — and **the rule is written into `ModalScreen`'s docblock** so the next modal does not have to guess. > **The through-line, and it is the same one as the design round two days earlier:** three of these four were a *mechanism* misread as a *judgement*. The Coach was not being unhelpful, it was leaking a sentinel. It did not decide the targets were unset, it had no eyes. The turn did not cost eight cents, it paid a one-time cache write. Each symptom invited a change to behaviour; each fix was one layer down.
>
> **1. The Coach emitted its own silence sentinel as a message.** A "COACH NOTICED" card read *"I'll read the current state and check for anything worth flagging."* then *"SKIP"*. `coach-pass.ts` asks for `SKIP` on an unremarkable day and detected it with `/^skip[.!]?$/i` — a whole-string match. The model wrote a preamble, called its tools, then said SKIP; the test failed and **the preamble plus the sentinel shipped as a proactive observation**.
> → **The second half was app-wide, not pass-only.** `runCoachTurn` accumulated text across *every* round trip, so on every tool-using turn in ordinary conversation the model's pre-tool narration was glued to the front of its answer — shown in the bubble, written to `ai_messages`, folded into the rolling thread summary, and replayed as history. The client now settles on the **post-tool** text, which is narration-free by construction: it was written before the tool results existed, so it cannot carry a conclusion drawn from them. The detector was hardened separately (last non-empty line, alone) because a prompt is never the first line of defence. `partialText` still carries the full transcript so an aborted turn keeps a lossless audit fragment.

### Known caveats (things that will bite if forgotten)
- ⚠️ **A `SafeAreaView` inside a React Native `Modal` gets a ZERO inset unless that Modal carries its own `SafeAreaProvider`** (found 2026-08-14, the capture round; the owner's report was *"the back button is not accessible because it is too high up on the screen"*). `SafeAreaView` in react-native-safe-area-context v5 is a **native view**, not a JS component reading React context: `RNCSafeAreaViewComponentView.findNearestProvider` walks `self.superview` for an `RNCSafeAreaProviderComponentView` and returns `self` when it finds none. An RN `Modal` on iOS presents its own `UIViewController`, so React context flows into it but the **native** chain does not pass through the provider react-navigation installs around the Stack. The `self` fallback can never become correct — it is read at `didMoveToWindow`/`finalizeUpdates` and refreshed only on a notification the *provider* posts, and unlike the provider it implements no `safeAreaInsetsDidChange` and has no "wait for a non-zero frame" retry. So it latches `UIEdgeInsetsZero` during the presentation and stays there, and any `pt-*` becomes the entire top offset. **Never fix this with a padding number** — put a `SafeAreaProvider` inside the Modal. `ModalScreen` (`src/components/ui/screen.tsx`) does it once, and **every full-screen Modal in this app must go through it**. All three chooser modals were wrong for months (Log sheet, Home's mode picker, exercise picker); the app-lock Modal escaped only because it centres its content and requests no inset. The general rule: **a native view's behaviour inside a `Modal` cannot be reasoned about from the React tree.**

- ✅ **FIXED 2026-08-07 — a one-off reminder no longer resurrects itself every day.** **The mechanism, corrected 2026-08-08** (three places, this one included, previously described it wrong — as a roll-forward re-applied on every resync; executing the pre-fix code disproves that, so do not restore the old wording): `reminderTrigger` used to resolve an *undated* one-off to **TODAY** at its clock time, and return null once that moment had passed. It therefore went **quiet for the rest of that day** — it was never rolled forward to tomorrow, and repeated resyncs within the same day changed nothing. The damage was **per-new-day**: the next day's first resync (app boot, or any Coach turn) re-resolved "today" against the new date, found a fresh future moment and scheduled it again. Nothing marks a one-off done but the user, so "remind me at 9am" fired every day, forever. The symptom was daily-forever; the cause was a day re-derived each new day, not a roll-forward. **The day is now resolved ONCE, at creation.** `createReminder` (`src/lib/db/repositories/reminders.ts`) stamps a concrete local `date` on a one-off that has a time and no date — today if that clock time is still ahead, tomorrow once it has passed, derived from `now`'s local Y/M/D through `todayISODate`, never `new Date('YYYY-MM-DD')`. That makes it a *dated* one-off, which reuses the already-correct dated branch: it fires at that moment and returns null on every resync afterwards. `reminderTrigger` therefore treats a still-undated one-off as unschedulable, and `set_reminder` returns the `date` it actually landed on so the Coach names the right day — and **the confirmation card names it too** (see the entry below). **Legacy undated rows: deliberately NO migration** — a single-user app whose reminder feature is barely used doesn't warrant one, and inventing a firing day for them at some arbitrary boot would re-guess exactly the day this change stopped guessing; they simply stop producing OS notifications and keep surfacing in-app. Lifecycle pinned in `db/reminders.test.mjs` §6 (including the legacy row, month/year rollover, and null on six later resyncs) and `db/coach-tools.test.mjs` §5.
- ✅ **DECIDED + SHIPPED 2026-08-08 — a one-off's date is a "NOT BEFORE" FLOOR for in-app surfacing, not an exact match.** Pinning the day (above) had a side effect nobody asked for: `isDueOn` returned true for a dated one-off *only on its exact date*, so a never-completed "remind me at 9am" vanished from every due-today surface — `get_today_snapshot.remindersDueToday`, `list_reminders.dueToday`, `generateDailyBrief` — from the day after its pinned day, permanently, while the row sat there `active`. Legacy *undated* rows kept surfacing forever, so the asymmetry ran backwards: the well-formed row disappeared and the malformed one nagged. **The semantics now, plainly:** a one-off is due in-app on its date **and on every day after it**, until the user completes or dismisses it; an undated one-off is due any day (the same rule with no floor); daily is always due; weekly is due on its anchor's weekday. Comparison is lexicographic on `YYYY-MM-DD`, which is chronological for that zero-padded shape. **This does not touch OS notifications.** `reminderTrigger` is a separate path and still lapses a passed one-off to null on every resync, so the in-app floor can never resurrect the notification bug — `db/reminders.test.mjs` §6(i) asserts exactly that pairing: a long-overdue one-off is still due in-app across three later days *and* yields null on five repeated resyncs, and completing it stops both. One consequence worth knowing: a **back-dated** one-off ("log the dose I missed on the 1st") is due the moment it is written, since its floor is already behind us.
- ✅ **FIXED 2026-08-08 — today's reminders can no longer be evicted from the Home brief by stale ones.** The floor above had a second consequence this list did not record: an un-dismissed one-off surfaces *forever*, and `listActiveReminders` sorts by **clock time only** — so a months-old 06:00 nudge outranked today's genuine daily and weekly reminders unconditionally, and the brief's `slice(0, 3)` handed the user three dead nudges instead of the day's plan. **The ordering rule now, plainly:** one shared ranked set (`dueRemindersFor(db, today)`, `src/lib/ai/insights.ts`) — **items due on their own day first, in their existing clock order; then overdue one-offs, oldest nag first**, with a source-index tiebreak so nothing depends on `Array.prototype.sort` stability. `daysOverdue` is > 0 **only** for a one-off with a pinned date strictly before today; recurring reminders and undated legacy one-offs are 0, because with no floor there is no age that can honestly be claimed. **In the brief** (`generateDailyBrief`) the set is split in two, so a carry-over can never masquerade as today's plan: **"On deck today: …"** carries only genuinely-today items (first 3 named, the rest counted — ", and 3 more", never silently truncated), and overdue ones get their own clause, **"Still open: Book bloodwork (09:00) — 4 mo overdue."** (first 2 named, rest counted). Ages are a hand-rolled `1 day / 12 days / 4 wk / 4 mo / 2 yr` — **Hermes has no `Intl`** — on the same thresholds as the Screenings ledger's span text. **In the Coach tool**, `get_today_snapshot.remindersDueToday` now projects `{ id, title, time, date, repeat, daysOverdue }`, capped at 10 with a sibling `remindersDueTodayOmitted` count that always ships; because the set is today-first the cap drops the stalest tail *first* — that is an ordering, not a guarantee today's own items survive, since more than 10 due on their own day will trim those as well, which the omitted count then reports. The tool description tells the model that `daysOverdue > 0` is a carried-over obligation it must not present as today's plan. Pinned by `db/insights.test.mjs` §§12–15 (§12 is the exact repro: five months-old one-offs plus a daily and a weekly). **Deliberately unchanged:** `isDueOn`, the notification path, and `list_reminders` — which returns *every* active reminder with a `dueToday` flag, unranked and uncapped, and carries no `daysOverdue`; it never truncates, so nothing can be hidden there. ⚠️ **Same defect, one surface over, still open:** the Coach screen's Reminders card (`src/components/coach/reminders-card.tsx` ← `useReminders` → `listActiveReminders`) renders every active reminder in clock order with no overdue distinction, so an old nudge sits interleaved with today's and gives no hint of its age. Nothing is dropped there, so it under-informs rather than misleads — pointing it at `dueRemindersFor` is the follow-up.
- ✅ **FIXED 2026-08-08 — the write-confirmation card now names the day it is about to pin.** Because a bare-time one-off gets a concrete day stamped at save time, approving *"Set reminder "Call the clinic" at 09:00"* at 22:00 silently wrote a row dated **tomorrow** — the card showed only the clock time, contradicting `write-tools.ts`'s own rule that the confirmation line must carry "what, how much, and (when not today) which day". The fix widened the shared contract rather than softening the rule: `CoachTool.confirmSummary` (`src/lib/ai/tools/types.ts`) takes a third argument — **the same `CoachToolContext` (`{ now }`) `execute` gets** — so a summary resolves the day off the same clock the write will use. (It was introduced as *optional*; it has since been made **required**. See the next entry for why that mattered.) `set_reminder`'s summary calls the very function `createReminder` calls (`resolveOneOffDay`, now exported from the reminders repository) and appends the day whenever it is not today: **`· tomorrow (Sat 8 Aug)`**, `· yesterday (…)`, or `· 2026-08-15 (Sat 15 Aug)` for anything else; a weekly's anchor is always printed as a day, never as "tomorrow". Weekday and month names are spelled out by hand — **Hermes has no `Intl`**, so `toLocaleDateString` is not available on device — and the ISO day is split componentwise, never `new Date('YYYY-MM-DD')`. Pinned in `db/coach-tools.test.mjs` §5 — five assertions: pins-to-tomorrow, silent-when-it-pins-to-today, an explicit ISO day printed with its weekday, a weekly anchor printed as a day, and an untimed one-off whose card is clock-independent because it derives no day at all.
- ✅ **FIXED 2026-08-08 — the card and the row now read ONE clock, so the approved card is the row that lands.** Widening `confirmSummary` (entry above) was only half the fix, and the previous version of this list got the leftover half wrong in two ways at once. **What was actually wrong:** the third argument shipped **optional**, and the one real call site (`src/lib/ai/coach-service.ts`) kept calling `confirmSummary(input, db)` while `execute` minted a *fresh* `new Date()` for itself afterwards — two clock reads with the user's approval sitting between them. **The divergence was never "an exact midnight boundary"**, and threading `{ now }` into the summary call alone would **not** have fixed it, because `execute` still read its own clock; do not restore either claim. It broke at **the reminder's own clock time**: a card rendered at 08:59:30 for "at 09:00" showed a bare time (meaning *today*), and approving it at 09:00:10 wrote a row dated **tomorrow** — the exact contradiction the card was added to prevent. **Now:** `streamCoachReply` holds one clock *factory* for the turn (`options.now ?? (() => new Date())`) and reads it **once per tool call**, above the confirmation gate; that single `CoachToolContext` object goes to both `confirmSummary` and `execute`, and the second `new Date()` is gone. **Per call, not per turn, deliberately** — a turn spans streaming, arbitrary approval latency and up to 8 model round-trips (`MAX_MODEL_CALLS_PER_TURN`), so freezing one instant for a whole turn would date a later `log_meal` to the wrong day across midnight; the window that must not move is card→row. The context argument is now **required**, making a card rendered without the turn clock a type error — optionality is precisely what let the widening sit inert and unnoticed. `db/coach-tools.test.mjs` **§16** drives the real `streamCoachReply` call site (scripted SSE stream; real registry, repositories and SQLite; the clock moved *inside* `confirmWrite` to simulate approval latency) and asserts the straddle writes **today**, that `execute` performed **no second clock read**, and — as a non-triviality control — that the same turn started 40 s later cards *and* writes **tomorrow**. **Three things to know, none of them defects being hidden:** (a) `set_reminder`'s notification check now runs against the card's instant, so in a straddle it can report a schedulable alert for a moment that has just passed — bounded by approval latency, and the OS is the final arbiter; if it ever matters the fix belongs in `src/lib/notifications/reminders.ts`, since schedulability is a live-OS question, not in the tool contract. (b) `write-tools.ts`'s `const now = context?.now ?? new Date();` inside `setReminderTool.confirmSummary` is now **vestigial** — unreachable from the app — and the comments around it still describe the argument as "optional"; a cleanup, not a bug. (c) `StreamOptions.now` is production surface that exists for the tests, matching the codebase's existing `fetchImpl` / `CoachToolContext.now` injection convention; the app never passes it.
- ✅ **DECIDED 2026-08-07 — the app's Documents directory stays PRIVATE: no `UIFileSharingEnabled`, no `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace`.** Export writes its JSON into Documents (`src/lib/export/export-file.ts` → `fs.Paths.document`), so the two Info.plist keys were added to `app.json` to surface that directory in the iOS Files app — then **reverted the same day**; `app.json`’s `ios.infoPlist` now carries only `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`. **The supported route for getting an export off the device is the `expo-sharing` share sheet**, which `export-file.ts` already prefers and which needs no Files-app exposure (`expo-sharing` is a dependency + plugin, and is in the owner's binary since 2026-08-25). **Correction to the reason first recorded here:** the live DB is *not* in Documents on the pinned version — verified 2026-08-07 against `@op-engineering/op-sqlite@17.1.2`, whose `ios/OPSQLite.mm` passes **`NSLibraryDirectory`** as the base path (Documents survives only as an exported constant, and no `OPSQLite_AppGroup` is set), while `src/lib/db/client.ts` calls `open({ name: 'arc.db' })` with **no `location`** — so `arc.db`, its `-wal`/`-shm` sidecars and the `.pre-migrate-*.bak` snapshots (`getDbPath()` + suffix) all sit under **Library/**. - ⚠️ **`src/lib/db/repositories/mission-generate.ts` was committed with a LITERAL NUL byte, which makes the file invisible to recursive search.** `planKey` (line ~181) joins a protocol id to an item title with U+0000 — a good delimiter, since it can't occur in either — but it was written as a raw `0x00` byte in the source instead of as an escape. Unchanged at runtime either way: any `planKey` dumped into a log, a test fixture, or an error message carries an embedded NUL and may print truncated or ragged.

## 3. Design & Styling — the Conformed Set

> **The philosophy in one line:** ARC is a working drawing set — the day is *drafted*, not listed.

**Adopted 2026-08-08, superseding Porcelain Ledger** (chosen 2026-07-24, shipped for two weeks). Where Porcelain Ledger was a printed lab report, this is the architect's set that produced it: the same calm and the same restraint, but **the container tells you what kind of thing it holds**. It leans on ARC's own name — *Architecture for Resilience & Continuity* — without ever making the user learn the metaphor.

Provenance: a six-set exploration, each set fully specified and hostile-reviewed for usability and anti-slop (89 findings), converged into this one, re-reviewed to **zero high findings**, then fixed and verified. The full trail is `docs/design-research/`; the spec of record is **`docs/design-research/implementation/00-design-spec.md`**, the RN port notes are `01-rn-port-guide.md`, what actually shipped is `02-migration-plan.md`, and the ADR is in `docs/decisions.md`. The earlier six directions (including Porcelain Ledger) are archived in `docs/design-directions.md` — read that before proposing any new one.

**How to make something look like ARC:** pick the block's *device* first, name it in the label voice, set its prose in serif and every measured value in mono, keep the corners square — and only reach for the accent if it is the one thing on the screen that matters.

Tokens live in `tailwind.config.js` (source of truth for every `className`) and are mirrored in `src/constants/theme.ts` for the few APIs that need literal colour strings. **Any palette change must touch both** — plus two files that hold their own copy and do *not* follow a Tailwind change: `app.json` (splash `backgroundColor`) and `app/(tabs)/_layout.tsx` (the tab bar, styled imperatively from `palette`).

> ⚠️ **This section was written as a specification, and it has since met hardware: four owner passes (2026-08-09/10/11/12) drove three rounds of fixes** — the deletions round, the devices-were-never-drawing round (marks restored as filled views, 2026-08-11), the hero round. Visual claims no pass has explicitly judged are flagged in "What is unverified" at the end of this section — read that before trusting them.

### The surface system — "devices" (the load-bearing idea)

This, not the palette, is the design. Under Porcelain Ledger every block was a card, so the container carried no information. Here **each kind of content gets its correct drafting device**, and hierarchy comes from the drawing vocabulary instead of arbitrary styling. The primitive is `<Block device="…">` (`src/components/ui/block.tsx`), in use at **168 device markings across 64 files** (162 of them JSX call sites) — plate 80 · margin 43 · well 18 · field 12 · grid 11 · stamp 4.

> **Counted 2026-08-12 (post-merge of the day's two rounds), re-run rather than re-derived:** `rg -o 'device="(plate|field|margin|grid|well|stamp)"' app src | wc -l` → **168**, the same pattern under `rg -l … | wc -l` → **64**, and narrowed to JSX — `rg -o '<Block device="(plate|field|margin|grid|well|stamp)"' app src` — **162**. The delta between the raw and JSX counts is prose mentions of `device="…"` inside comments. *(These figures read 138 / 58 / 135 from 2026-08-09, and 155 / 63 / 149 for a few hours mid-day on 2026-08-12 — the day's second round moved them again before the first measurement was even committed, which is the whole argument in one datum. A hand-checked figure goes stale in a single commit — re-run the command before quoting this paragraph.)*

> ⚠️ **RE-AMENDED 2026-08-11. The 2026-08-09 amendment below is WITHDRAWN — all six devices draw their marks again.** The chat thread staying off the `well` stands; that half was unrelated. Full reasoning in the 2026-08-11 ADR (`docs/decisions.md`), and the short version is that the three "unmarked" devices were not communicating badly, they were **rendering as boxes**: every one of their marks was written as a one-sided border width against a whole-element border colour, the pair React Native paints as a complete rectangle. Every mark is now a **filled view**.

| Content | Device | Treatment **as shipped** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Schedules, ledgers, record lists (mission, Data trends, Settings rows, Protocols, Screenings) | **`plate`** — a record is a table | `border border-hairline bg-paper-hi px-3.5 py-3`, ruled rows inside |
| Readiness / status verdict | **`field`** — a verdict, not a box | `relative px-3 py-3` + 11pt L-shaped corner ticks at top-left / bottom-right, each drawn as **two filled bars** (`CornerTicks`) |
| Prose (Coach brief, rationale) | **`margin`** — an annotation in the margin | A 2pt `bg-hairline` column beside the content, indented `pl-3`. **Structural, not a class** — a `border-l-2` here is the bug |
| Metric grids | **`grid`** — the grid *is* the object | No outer box. Rules run **between** cells, drawn by `GridCell` — a leading `Divider` per cell and a `VerticalDivider` conditioned on a cell actually following, so an odd count can't rule off into empty space |
| Capture surfaces (the Log command field, the Coach composer) | **`well`** — recessed stock | `border border-paper-deep bg-paper-dim px-3.5 py-3` |
| The one next action | **`stamp`** | `border-[1.5px] border-pine bg-paper-hi px-4 py-4`, plus an opt-in `cap` — the 3pt accent/ink barber hatch over the top edge (`.cf-card--accent::before`). Accent cards and the Coach revision card take it; **Home's hero does not**, because `.cf-hero` is a separate class on the sheet with the same border and no cap |

**~~Why they stopped drawing.~~ Why they came back — and the paragraph this replaces is worth understanding, because it was wrong in an expensive way.** It read: the owner's first note on hardware was *"there are some weird boxes and lines in some places, notably the metrics and coach brief on the home screen, but there are more"*; that is the surface system reading as noise rather than structure; §5 says drafting chrome pays rent or goes; so the ticks, the margin rule and the between-cell hairlines were cut.

Every step of that is sound except the premise. Read the sentence again and go and look at what the two named surfaces were made of. **The metrics** are `device="grid"`, whose cells drew `border-t border-hairline` + `border-r border-hairline`. **The coach brief** is `device="margin"`, which drew `border-l-2 border-hairline`. The `field`'s ticks were 11×11 views carrying `border-l border-t`. All three are a one-sided width against a whole-element colour — the shape that drops React Native onto its border-bitmap path and **paints all four edges**. The margin rule came out as a box round the brief. The cell rules came out as a box round each metric. The corner ticks came out as two small boxes floating at opposite corners. The owner was reporting a rendering fault and naming the two worst offenders; it was read as a design verdict on the system those surfaces belong to.

The marks are back, drawn as filled views, which is how everything else in this app draws a rule (`Divider`, and the reasoning above it in `src/components/ui/block.tsx`). **The lesson, for the second time in this project after the paper grid: establish that a thing draws before concluding anything about how it looks.**

**The Coach thread takes no device.** It was a `well` on the reading "a capture surface, the turns are marks on it". On hardware that read as a conversation boxed for no reason, and the reading was wrong anyway: a well is for a surface you capture *into*, and that is the composer, which wears it already. The thread is not a record filed on the page — it **is** the page, so it sits on the sheet with no device and no section label; only the turns are drawn (`app/(tabs)/coach.tsx`, `src/components/coach/message-bubble.tsx`).

**Rule: a block gets exactly one device, and devices NEVER nest.** No plate inside a plate, no field inside a stamp. If a section seems to need two, it is two sections. (A plain `<View>` used for layout is not a device and may sit anywhere.) This is **enforced at runtime in `__DEV__`** — a nested Block logs a `console.error` naming both devices — because prose was the only guard on a primitive that gets copied across ~40 screens.

One trap the primitive's own doc comment records, and the reason to read it before writing a new surface:

- ~~**`grid`: the last cell takes no trailing rule.**~~ **Moot since 2026-08-09** — the `grid` device draws no rules at all, so there is no trailing rule to get wrong. (It used to matter: "between cells" means a rule needs a cell on *both* sides, and the obvious two-column implementation gives every even-indexed cell a `border-r`, which with an odd count draws a rule into empty space off the final cell. The port guide's snippet and the mockup's CSS both carry that flaw.)
- **`well`: either the block IS the field, or the field IS the well — never both.** A capture surface is either a `well` wrapping a *bare* `TextInput`, or a group of inputs each wearing `border-paper-deep bg-paper-dim` with no block at all. A well *containing* an input that has its own surface stacks two recesses, and the only way to keep the inner one legible is to raise it onto plate stock — which inverts the whole system. **The rule to check a diff against: an input is never `bg-paper-hi`.** This reaches every control, not only text fields; it does *not* reach content (a Coach turn is a raised `paper-hi` slip wherever it lies, and it stayed raised when the thread came off the well).

### Colour

Ground is a neutral drafting bone. **Contrast, measured.** `ink-muted` clears 4.5:1 on paper (5.97), paper-hi (6.84) and paper-dim (5.17) — but **fails on `paper-deep` (4.21)**. No screen puts it there today and none should: on `paper-deep` the metadata voice is `ink-secondary` (5.83).

**The rules themselves are the one thing in this palette that does NOT clear its threshold — recorded here rather than omitted.** Text carries a 4.5:1 floor, but WCAG 1.4.11 puts a separate **3:1** floor on non-text visuals, and `hairline` `#A9A28E` draws every plate edge and every row separator in the app. Measured 2026-08-09 against the surfaces it actually sits on: **paper-hi 2.29:1 · paper 2.00:1** (and paper-dim 1.73, paper-deep 1.41). All four are under 3:1. `hairline-soft` `#C6C1B0` is weaker still (1.62 on paper-hi, 1.42 on paper) but has no consumers — see the token table. Two adjacent numbers, since a boundary is only as visible as its weakest edge: the **`well` device's border** `paper-deep` vs the page reads **1.42:1**, and the fill step that also signals a recess (`paper-dim` vs `paper`) reads **1.15:1**.

**The decision, stated explicitly so the numbers above are not mistaken for a pass:**

- **Plate borders, row separators and the `margin` rule are judged decorative and exempt, and they stay at `#A9A28E`.** 1.4.11 covers visuals *required* to identify a control or understand content. These enclose text that is itself at 5.97:1 or better; no value, state or action becomes unavailable if the rule is invisible, and the alternative — a rule dark enough to pass — is precisely the heavy furniture the de-boxing pass removed. **The honest tension, recorded rather than argued away:** this section calls the surface system "the load-bearing idea" *because the container encodes what it holds*, which is exactly the claim a decorative exemption denies. Both cannot be fully true. The reconciliation is that the device conveys **emphasis and kind**, never unique information — every block also names itself in the label voice — so a reader who cannot see the rule loses nuance, not content.
- **The `well` boundary at 1.42:1 is NOT covered by that argument and is an open item**, because a well marks an *input* — a UI component, which 1.4.11 covers by name. It is not being changed sight-unseen: the recess reads as much from its fill as from its border, and no device has rendered this system yet. **Judge it on the first device review, together with the rest of §3.**
- **If the device review says the rules read as mush, the follow-up is a darker `hairline`, not a heavier one.** The measured landing point: `#7E7767` clears 3:1 on both real surfaces (**4.01 on paper-hi, 3.50 on paper**) at the same 1px weight. `#847D6C` is the marginal option (3.69 / 3.22). Any such change is a palette change and must touch `tailwind.config.js` **and** `src/constants/theme.ts` together.

Every ratio in this section (and in the two token files) is WCAG 2.x relative luminance, recomputed 2026-08-09 rather than copied: `L = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B` over sRGB channels linearised at the 0.03928 knee, `(L₁+0.05)/(L₂+0.05)`. All 20+ figures previously asserted here reproduced to the stated precision; the hairline row is the one that had never been written down.

#### Biological signals — TWO CUTS PER STATE, and they are not interchangeable

| State | `signal-{state}` — the **SWATCH** | `signal-{state}-ink` — the **TEXT** cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| optimal | `#2E8B57` | `#185A36` |
| good | `#2C6C95` | `#24567A` |
| caution | `#A97B22` | `#6E4F15` |
| poor | `#AA402C` | `#8F3524` |
| unknown | `#5C5340` — the metadata ink itself. An absent reading is absent, not a state, so it needs no separate cut. | (none) |

- **The swatch is for fills and icons**, where the 3:1 non-text threshold applies. It is **not a text colour and never was**: as text on `paper-hi` the four swatches measure **3.82 / 5.13 / 3.41 / 5.44** — two fail outright, and the two that pass do so by luck of hue.
- **The ink cut is the same hue darkened until it clears 4.5:1.** On paper: 6.46 / 6.14 / 5.91 / 6.11. On paper-hi: 7.40 / 7.04 / 6.77 / 7.00. On paper-dim: 5.60 / 5.32 / 5.12 / 5.29. On **paper-deep only `optimal` clears (4.56)**; the other three land at 4.17–4.34, so **signal text does not belong on `paper-deep`**.
- **Which to reach for:** anything read as words or digits takes the ink cut. A glyph that carries its meaning by *shape* as well as colour — an icon — may stay on the swatch. Reaching for the swatch to colour a value is the most likely way to fail contrast in this system.

> **The firewall rule (sacred), and it runs BOTH ways.** Signal colours mark **biological state only** — pillars, freshness, biomarker states, overdue screenings. Never interface chrome. Conversely **the accent never marks biology.** This was a finding in all six hostile reviews. It is also why the accent is petrol: redline would have forced `poor` to umber `#7A4A1E`, because crimson chrome and rust biology read as one hue at swatch size — permanent strain on the firewall in a product whose worst health state is red. The firewall reaches the nav theme too: React Navigation's `notification` slot (tab-bar badges — pure chrome) takes the accent, **not** `signal.caution` as it did under Porcelain.

> **The accent budget (a ceiling, not a quota).** The accent appears only on: the Home hero, **one** primary action per screen, completion stamps, the user's own chat bubbles, the active tab, and the Coach presence dot. **Settings and reference surfaces carry ZERO accent** — the read-only Experiments screens are the worked example: a "Conclude" button there would invite a verdict with no numbers behind it. If everything is emphasised, nothing is directive.

### Light only — by decision, not omission

ARC is **light-mode only**: `userInterfaceStyle: "light"` in app.json, an unconditional nav theme in `app/_layout.tsx`, `StatusBar style="dark"`, and **zero `dark:` variants anywhere in the codebase** (a grep for `dark:` in app/ and src/ should return nothing — if it returns something, someone broke the system). Paper is the identity. This survives the restyle unchanged. If ARC ever needs a night mode, the archived **Night Watch (B)** direction is the designed candidate — a second complete theme, not `dark:` variants bolted onto this one.

### Typography — three voices

| Voice | Stack | Used for | Class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Label** | Avenir Next Condensed → Helvetica Neue → system-ui → sans-serif | Uppercase section labels, eyebrows, chips, and **every button at every weight** | `font-label` |
| **Serif** | Iowan Old Style → Palatino → Georgia | Prose — briefs, Coach turns, why-lines, item titles | `font-serif` |
| **Mono** | Menlo → Courier New → monospace | **Every measured value** — times, counters, versions, metrics, dimension strings | `font-mono` |

**"Serif speaks, mono measures."** A standalone measurement set in the label or serif face is a bug. **No `Text` may be faceless** — a tracked-caps label left in the default system face is the same bug as a measurement left out of mono, and the label voice is the one the app had been shipping without entirely.

**"Buttons" means every button.** Filled primary, outlined secondary and bare text buttons all take the label voice, as do chips: the face is what makes a control read as a control. Weight is expressed by **size and casing, never by face** — full-width primary actions at ~15px sentence case, compact and inline actions at 11–13px uppercase with ~1.2px tracking. The one exception is a measured value *inside* a label (`v3`, `280 g`, `12 sets`), which stays mono.

Shared primitive: `SectionLabel` (`src/components/ui/section-label.tsx`) — the label voice at 10px with an optional right-aligned **mono** note for a tally. Several screens still define their own local `SectionLabel`; sweeping them onto the shared one is a separate pass.

> ⚠️ **Font-stack gotcha (carried forward — it cost an hour once already):** define `fontFamily` in tailwind.config.js as a plain **array** of family names. Do NOT use `nativewind/theme`'s `platformSelect` — its custom-function CSS syntax cannot carry a family name containing spaces, and "Iowan Old Style" silently compiled to an **empty declaration** (verified in the bundle's style registry). Plain CSS stacks parse correctly; native walks the list and picks the first family it has.

> ⚠️ **These are iOS-native substitutions, not the mockup's faces.** The mockup was authored in a browser on Windows and specified **Bahnschrift SemiCondensed** (label) and **Constantia** (serif); **neither exists on iOS.** Shipping a real face via `expo-font` remains available if the label voice reads generic on hardware. Do not copy the Windows stacks back in.

### Geometry & rhythm

- **Corners are square.** `rounded-card` is mapped to **`0px`** rather than deleted. It is a 🩹 shim with **zero consumers as of 2026-08-09** — the "so any surviving usage flattens automatically" framing this line used to carry implied there were survivors; there are not, the sweep took them all. Buttons take `rounded-btn` (2px) at most. This is the visible departure from Porcelain Ledger's 10px cards.
- **No shadows, no elevation, no glow, no gradients — anywhere.** Layering is borders plus the `paper` / `paper-hi` / `paper-dim` triad.
- **Rules enclose objects, never pages** (owner call, 2026-07-24 — survives the restyle intact). A hairline is correct on a **plate edge** and **between rows of one list**, because both times it draws the boundary of a single object. A rule laid across the page to separate two sections is furniture: one above a short block and one below it draws a box around it. **Sections are separated by whitespace only.**
- **Tap targets ≥ 44pt.** Single-line rows need an explicit min-height.
- **Text floor: 10px by rule — and it is now BREACHED at three sites.** Re-measured 2026-08-12 over `app/` + `src/`: the smallest type in the app is **`text-[9px]`, at 3 occurrences**, all shipped by the 2026-08-11/12 rounds — `src/components/coach/message-bubble.tsx` (the folio date, mono), `app/workout-live.tsx:99` and `app/workout-import.tsx:437` (label-voice eyebrows). `text-[10px]` sits at **157**, no named Tailwind size below `text-sm` (14px) is in use, and the one imperative size (`app/(tabs)/_layout.tsx`, the tab labels) is still 10. The floor rule stands in the spec, so the three 9px sites are an **open item — lift them to 10px or amend the rule**; what must not happen is a fourth site citing these three as precedent. *(Until 2026-08-12 this line read "the smallest type anywhere is exactly `text-[10px]` at 138 occurrences, and nothing is smaller" — true when measured on 2026-08-09, broken by the two rounds since; the doc round caught it, not the rounds that broke it.)* Command: `rg -o 'text-\[[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)?px\]' app src | sort | uniq -c | sort -k2 -V`.
- Section rhythm on Home: `mt-5`–`mt-9`; airy density is part of the calm and, since the de-boxing pass, the *only* thing separating sections.
- One shared container: `Screen` (`src/components/ui/screen.tsx`) — safe-area, `px-5` gutter, `bg-paper`.
- **Ionicons only**, and no new dependency: the corner ticks and the diamond markers are bordered / rotated Views precisely so `react-native-svg` stays out of the tree.

### Honesty rules (design requirements, not copy suggestions — each was a real hostile-review finding)

- **Ledgers must sum to their own totals.** If the Today card says 2,180 kcal, the visible meals must add to 2,180.
- **Tallies must reconcile.** "3 of 11" with a fold means folded + visible = 11, and *skipped ≠ done*.
- **Empty is authored, never blank.** "No reading yet", "Import labs to populate", "Nothing logged yet today."
- **No data, no number.** A metric with no source is an em-dash — never a plausible-looking estimate. No denominators until targets exist.
- **A pending write is a live decision.** The confirmation card is the *last* thread object, its consequence in future tense ("On approve: v2 is written; v1 is kept"), nothing after it, composer disabled. Never draw a decision and its outcome simultaneously.
- **No invented reference codes.** No sheet numbers, tile keys or designator badges that key no user action. Drafting chrome must pay rent or go — which is why the mockup's desk background, registration marks and title blocks do **not** ship.
- **Product nouns, not conceit vocabulary.** It is "Today's Mission", not "Issue Schedule". The user should never have to learn the metaphor.

### Patterns worth reusing

- **Pick the device first.** The container is the first decision, before any styling — it is what says what kind of thing the reader is looking at.
- **Derived emphasis:** the hero isn't authored separately — it's the first unresolved mission item (`src/lib/home/derive-mission.ts`). One source of truth, so the UI can't contradict itself.
- **Whole-string class maps** for dynamic styles (`src/components/home/signal.tsx` — its SPOKEN / CONDITION / MARK maps are the reference for encoding state without relying on hue). Tailwind only sees class names that appear literally in source — **never build `bg-signal-${level}`**.
- **Explicit hairlines**, not `divide-*` — that utility needs a CSS sibling selector RN doesn't have.

### What is unverified, and what was deliberately not done

**⚠️ SEEN ON DEVICE, PARTIALLY JUDGED.** Four owner passes (2026-08-09/10/11/12) have rendered the system on hardware and driven three fix rounds — but automated verification remains `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npx eslint app src`, Prettier and the headless suites, none of which is evidence about how anything *looks*, and the web preview stays a logic-check surface only. The marks restored on 2026-08-11 have not been explicitly re-judged since. **Three things are first-of-kind in this tree and fail SILENTLY when unsupported:**

- **`Avenir Next Condensed`** — the label voice. Inside `font-label` it heads a CSS stack, so native falls through to Helvetica Neue if it is missing. **But `app/(tabs)/_layout.tsx` sets `fontFamily` imperatively as a single string, and React Native takes no fallback list there** — if iOS lacks that exact family, the five tab labels drop to the system face while every other label in the app renders correctly. If that happens, set it to `'Helvetica Neue'` (also iOS-native, next in the stack) rather than leaving it unset.
- **`border-dashed`** (`app/protocol-versions.tsx`) — the proposed/suspended version marker. First dashed border in the app.
- **Rotated-square diamond markers** (`app/screenings.tsx`, `transform: [{ rotate: '45deg' }]`) — the horizon-axis terminals. First transform used as a visual mark.

This restyle added no dependency, so it needed no EAS build **of its own** — and the owner passes did judge it on the existing binary (the "cannot be judged until the pending build ships" claim this line used to carry was wrong: that build gated the dormant *native modules*, not the styling). What still awaits confirmation is exactly the list in this subsection.

**The type SCALE sweep was NOT done, and that is a deliberate call, not an oversight.** The three type *voices* ship, because a voice is a `fontFamily` token the whole app inherits. The *scale* does not: **`tailwind.config.js` defines no `fontSize` or `letterSpacing` tokens, and 74 files still carry arbitrary type values** — **876** arbitrary `text-[Npx]` and **213** arbitrary `tracking-[…]` occurrences.
